I've been meaning to start a blog for years. Every time I'd learn something interesting — a subtle bug, a design pattern that finally clicked, a tool I underestimated — I'd think "I should write this down." Then I wouldn't.

This is my attempt to actually do it.

## What this is

A digital notebook, mostly. When I figure something out, I'll write it here. When I build something, I'll document the decisions. When I have a take on something, I'll put it down in writing and see if it holds up.

I'm not trying to build a tutorial site or an authority blog. Those already exist and they're good. What I can offer is my specific perspective — the things that confused me, the dead ends I walked down, the "oh, that's what's actually happening" moments.

The goal is to write the post I'd want to find when I Google the same problem six months from now.

## What I'll write about

Software engineering, mostly. Distributed systems, web development, things I've built, things that broke, things I figured out the hard way. I'll also write about the meta-skills: how I learn, how I approach problems, how I think about building products.

I'm drawn to the intersection of writing and building — the idea that a developer who can explain their thinking clearly is a fundamentally different (and more valuable) kind of engineer than one who can't.

## Why write publicly

There's the obvious selfish angle: writing forces you to understand something properly. If I can't explain it clearly, I probably don't understand it as well as I thought. Public writing sharpens that feedback loop.

There's also a longer game. The developers I've learned the most from aren't the ones with the biggest audiences — they're the ones who wrote clearly and consistently about things they actually cared about. That compounds. A post you write today might be the most useful thing someone reads in two years.

And honestly, I just want to have done it. I want to look back in a year and see a trail of things I was curious about, built, and thought through.

## What this isn't

A promise of frequency. I'll write when I have something worth saying. I'd rather publish ten sharp posts than fifty filler pieces.

It's also not a brand exercise. I'm not optimizing for followers or SEO or any of that. I'm writing for the version of me who needed to read this six months ago.

Welcome. Hope something here is useful.
